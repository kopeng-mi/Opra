Produce exactly one image: a flat rectangular texture map, to be wrapped onto a 3D surface in a game engine. It is a flat picture of a surface and nothing else. It is not an object, not a sphere, not a scene, not a photograph of anything with edges or depth.

Subject. The whole surface of a small scorched rocky planet that orbits very close to its star. Dark basalt plains in charcoal and deep brown, vast overlapping impact basins, long radial crack systems, and broad fields of solidified lava flow in near-black. Scattered bright ejecta rays streaking out from the youngest craters in pale grey. Sun-baked, cracked and utterly dry. No water, no ice, no vegetation, no clouds.

Layout. One image, exactly twice as wide as it is tall, in equirectangular projection: the surface of a whole sphere unrolled flat into a rectangle. The left and right edges must match exactly so they join without a seam when wrapped around a sphere. Features stretch and smear horizontally as they approach the top and bottom edges, which are the north and south poles. Fill the entire rectangle edge to edge.

Lighting. Completely flat and even, as though lit from every direction at once. There must be no sun, no directional light, no cast shadows, no terminator, no day and night division, no specular highlights, and no darkening toward any edge. This image records surface colour only. Lighting is added later by the engine, so any lighting baked in here would be wrong twice over.

Do not include: a sphere, a globe, a planet seen from space, a curved horizon, any three-dimensional object, black space, stars, a starfield, a nebula, atmosphere, glow, a border, a frame, a vignette, a drop shadow, text, letters, numbers, labels, watermarks, or a colour chart.
